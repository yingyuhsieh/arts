The Donne Triptych places a family before the Virgin and Child. Sir John Donne kneels on the left. His wife Elizabeth and a daughter appear on the right, accompanied by Saints Catherine and Barbara. John the Baptist and John the Evangelist occupy the wings. Notice the figures’ scale. Donors and saints share a comprehensible setting, giving prayer the form of an encounter. The tiled floor and open architecture help connect the panels. Rich textiles and distant scenery support the central gathering without dispersing our attention. The National Gallery’s technical research also reveals changes to the couple’s faces during painting. Memling adjusted their appearance toward greater individuality. The work shows how a devotional image can combine ideal figures with the recognizable features of the people who commissioned it.



[Hans_Memling_s_Conquest_slide_0016.png]
Completed in 1479, the St John Altarpiece was commissioned for the church of St John’s Hospital in Bruges. Saints gather around the Virgin and Child. The infant gives Catherine a ring, presenting the traditional subject of her spiritual marriage to Christ. The left wing depicts the Baptist’s martyrdom. On the right, John the Evangelist receives an apocalyptic vision on Patmos. A stable central gathering stands beside dramatic events. The background also contains a connection to hospital life. Between the columns, a wooden crane recalls the equipment used to lift wine barrels in Bruges. Museum research links income from the wine trade with the hospital. This small feature joins the sacred scene to the city’s economy and to the institution that paid for the work.
