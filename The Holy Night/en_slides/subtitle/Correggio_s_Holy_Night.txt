Origins of the Holy Light

[Correggio_s_Holy_Night_slide_0014.png]
So you might be wondering, where did he get the idea to paint the baby Jesus as a literal glowing light bulb in the dark?
Well, the crucial point is this.
If you read the classic Christmas story in the Gospel of Luke,
it mentions a glorious light accompanying the angels when they visit the shepherds.
But nowhere does it actually say the baby itself was glowing in the manger.
So, Correggio's glowing infant isn't just a word-for-word illustration of the Gospel.

[Correggio_s_Holy_Night_slide_0015.png]
Instead, he's drawing on this incredibly rich tapestry of outside literature, mystical visions, and older art traditions.
The idea of a blinding light in the Nativity Cave traces all the way back to an early, non-canonical text called the Protevangelium of James.
Later on, in the 14th century, St. Bridget of Sweden described experiencing a famous vision of a radiant Christ child.
And that actually inspired northern European painters like Hugo van der Goes to start painting glowing infants decades before Correggio even picked up a brush.
It also perfectly aligns with the theology in the Gospel of John, which famously refers to Jesus as the light of the world.
