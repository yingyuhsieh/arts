# Jean Fouquet | YouTube Video Content Plan (English)

## A. Core Narrative

A king, a royal treasurer, and a prayer book pulled apart: Jean Fouquet gave visible form to power and devotion in fifteenth-century France. Begin with the weighty red portrait of Charles VII, reunite the two wings of the Melun Diptych on screen, and move through intimate illuminated pages and royal history. End by asking how works scattered among collections can be seen together again.

## D. Detailed Segment Plan

### 1. Hook: a king without a crown
- **Purpose:** Open with a visual surprise about royal authority.
- **Key message:** Charles VII needs no crown to dominate the picture; red fabric, curtains, and a solid body do the work.
- **Content:** Start on a tight crop of red cloth and reveal the king's face. Viewers may expect a throne or regalia, but Fouquet builds authority through stillness, scale, and the presence of a particular person. This portrait belongs to a France rebuilding after prolonged war. The film follows the same artist as he shapes the identities of kings, officials, and worshippers. Avoid presenting the painting as a record of a specific political ceremony.
- **Artwork / people / place:** *Portrait of Charles VII*, Louvre; Charles VII.
- **Visual assets:** A01 full view, robe detail, face, curtains (four shots).
- **Source page:** https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl010064847
- **Direct image / rights:** See A01 in E.
- **Sequence:** Red detail → face → full painting → title.

### 2. Italy and the court portrait
- **Purpose:** Establish Fouquet's travel and his approach to portraiture.
- **Key message:** He painted a pope in Rome; surviving portraits combine close observation with formal order.
- **Content:** Fouquet travelled to Italy in the 1440s and painted Pope Eugene IV. That original is lost: a later copy must never be shown as if it were the original. Back in France, Charles VII's physical presence and Guillaume Jouvenel des Ursins's prayerful pose show two forms of elite authority. His Italian experience helps frame his treatment of space, though no single visual detail can be securely traced to that journey alone.
- **Artwork / people / place:** Lost *Portrait of Pope Eugene IV*; A01, A02; Rome and Tours.
- **Visual assets:** A01 full view; A02 full view, folded hands, architectural setting (four).
- **Source pages:** https://arts-graphiques.louvre.fr/detail/artistes/0/1016-FOUQUET-Jean ; https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl010064846
- **Direct image / rights:** See E.
- **Sequence:** Place labels → portrait comparison → A02 details.

### 3. Left wing: a donor enters sacred space
- **Purpose:** Explain devotion and personal patronage.
- **Key message:** Étienne Chevalier kneels beside his patron Saint Stephen, inserting an identifiable patron into a sacred image.
- **Content:** The Melun Diptych's left wing is now in Berlin. Chevalier, a royal financial official, kneels as Saint Stephen presents him toward the other panel. The saint holds a stone recalling his martyrdom. Let the viewer read their poses, then follow their gaze beyond the frame. A photograph of the single panel preserves its figures, but misses the exchange with the Madonna once seen opposite it.
- **Artwork / people / place:** A03; Gemäldegalerie, Berlin; Chevalier and Saint Stephen.
- **Visual assets:** A03 full view, kneeling figure, saint and stone, gaze toward the right (four).
- **Source page:** https://search.smb.museum/object/obj-865553?page=2&sammlungen=Gem%C3%A4ldegalerie
- **Direct image / rights:** See A03 in E.
- **Sequence:** Figures → full panel → extend the line of sight → reveal right wing.

### 4. Right wing: Madonna, angels, and artist
- **Purpose:** Read the diptych's striking contrast and its maker's signature.
- **Key message:** Red and blue angels encircle a pale Madonna; a small enamel portrait once placed Fouquet on the original frame.
- **Content:** The right wing is in Antwerp. The almost sculptural Madonna and Child face the donor across the divide. Saturated red and blue angels make this sacred space seem startlingly unreal. The exposed breast belongs within the iconographic tradition of the nursing Madonna. The popular identification of her face with Agnès Sorel remains a proposal, not a proven likeness. Fouquet's enamel self-portrait medallion, formerly on the frame, is now in the Louvre; it was not painted at the centre of either panel.
- **Artwork / people / place:** A04, A05; Antwerp and Paris.
- **Visual assets:** A04 full view, angels, Madonna and Child; A05 medallion (four).
- **Source pages:** https://kmska.be/nl/meesterwerk/madonna-omringd-door-serafijnen-en-cherubijnen ; https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl010115375
- **Direct image / rights:** See E.
- **Sequence:** Reunited wings → right panel → angels → medallion.

### 5. The prayer book pulled apart
- **Purpose:** Shift from panels to private reading.
- **Key message:** Fouquet made monumental visual spaces on small pages, though the book's present fragments must be understood as part of a once continuous object.
- **Content:** The *Hours of Étienne Chevalier* was a book for private prayer before it was dismantled. The Musée Condé in Chantilly preserves forty surviving leaves, not the book's original total. Use a miniature of the donor and his patron saint to echo the diptych's left wing. Then briefly introduce the *Hours of Simon de Varie*: multiple illuminators worked on this manuscript, so each page's attribution must be checked. Across formats, Fouquet gives figures, architecture, and light a remarkable sense of scale.
- **Artwork / people / place:** A06, A07; Chantilly, the Getty, and KB The Hague.
- **Visual assets:** A06 whole page, figures, architecture; A07 verified leaf (four).
- **Source pages:** https://chateaudechantilly.fr/categ-collection/manuscrits/les-heures-detienne-chevalier-par-jean-fouquet/ ; https://www.getty.edu/art/collection/object/103RW9
- **Direct image / rights:** See E; A07 image clearance pending.
- **Sequence:** Page turn → A06 and details → map of scattered leaves → A07.

### 6. Giving history an order
- **Purpose:** Move from private devotion to public stories of monarchy.
- **Key message:** Chronicle and chivalric imagery turn political power into legible arrangements and ceremony.
- **Content:** In the *Grandes Chroniques de France*, Fouquet depicts Charles V entertaining Emperor Charles IV in 1378. The illumination dates instead to around 1455–60: this is a later construction of history, not an eyewitness sketch. A miniature of Louis XI presiding over the Order of Saint Michael, around 1470, likewise places the king within a carefully ordered court. Both images invite a question beyond what happened: how should monarchy be seen?
- **Artwork / people / place:** A08, A09; Bibliothèque nationale de France.
- **Visual assets:** A08 full page and table centre; A09 full page and king with knights (four).
- **Source pages:** https://classes.bnf.fr/fouquet/pages/scene.htm ; https://classes.bnf.fr/fouquet/grand/f129.htm
- **Direct image / rights:** See E; A09 pending.
- **Sequence:** Date cards “1378” and “c. 1455–60” → banquet → chapter scene → composition comparison.

### 7. Beyond portraiture: mourning and battle
- **Purpose:** Show devotional emotion and later narrative ambition.
- **Key message:** A large religious panel and a battle miniature extend Fouquet's range, with careful attribution language.
- **Content:** The *Pietà of Nouans* in the church of Saint Martin uses a broad group, restrained expressions, and a horizontal design to produce a different emotional register from the court portraits. Introduce it as “attributed to Fouquet.” Then inspect the Louvre miniature commonly called the *Battle of Cannae*: ordered ranks and dense details recast an ancient event in fifteenth-century visual language. Other hands may be responsible for parts of the manuscript; do not attribute its ornamental border wholesale to Fouquet.
- **Artwork / people / place:** A10, A11; Nouans-les-Fontaines and the Louvre.
- **Visual assets:** A10 whole and figure details; A11 whole and army details (four).
- **Source pages:** https://pop.culture.gouv.fr/notice/palissy/PM37000342 ; https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl020214911
- **Direct image / rights:** See E; A11 pending.
- **Sequence:** Slow move across A10 → pause → quicker pan across A11 → full miniature.

### 8. Closing: reassembling Fouquet's world
- **Purpose:** Return to separation and original relationships.
- **Key message:** Panels, medallion, and manuscript leaves survive in different collections; reconnecting them brings their first viewing contexts into focus.
- **Content:** Put the two Melun wings and the former frame medallion together on screen, then return to Chevalier in his prayer book and to the royal chronicle. Fouquet gives a king physical weight, a patron a place in sacred space, and historical episodes an exact visual order. Ask viewers which detail they would most like to inspect at full size and direct them to the collection links. Label all current locations; the digital reconstruction must not suggest that the pieces remain assembled in their original frame.
- **Artwork / people / place:** A01, A03–A06, A08.
- **Visual assets:** Fresh crops of each work and a labelled reconstruction (six).
- **Source pages:** https://www.smb.museum/en/museums-institutions/kulturforum/exhibitions/detail/jean-fouquet-the-melun-diptych/ ; https://chateaudechantilly.fr/categ-collection/manuscrits/les-heures-detienne-chevalier-par-jean-fouquet/
- **Direct image / rights:** See E.
- **Sequence:** Wings together → three collection labels → prayer-book page → king → closing question.

## G. Fact-Checking Notes

1. Use approximately 1420 to 1478–81 for Fouquet's life, without a precise birth or death date; the Roman papal portrait is lost. Source: https://arts-graphiques.louvre.fr/detail/artistes/0/1016-FOUQUET-Jean
2. The Melun wings and frame medallion are now in three collections. Institutional dates differ, so “c. 1450s” is the safest umbrella date. Agnès Sorel's identification is unproven. Sources: https://www.smb.museum/en/museums-institutions/kulturforum/exhibitions/detail/jean-fouquet-the-melun-diptych/ ; https://www.kmska.be/en/6-things-you-need-know-about-fouquets-madonna
3. Chantilly's forty surviving leaves are not the original total for the dismantled *Hours of Étienne Chevalier*. Source: https://chateaudechantilly.fr/categ-collection/manuscrits/les-heures-detienne-chevalier-par-jean-fouquet/
4. Several artists worked on the *Hours of Simon de Varie*; check page-level attribution. Source: https://www.getty.edu/art/collection/object/103RW9
5. The chronicle banquet depicts 1378 but was painted around 1455–60; the Nouans panel is presented as attributed to Fouquet. Sources: https://classes.bnf.fr/fouquet/pages/scene.htm ; https://pop.culture.gouv.fr/notice/palissy/PM37000342
6. Another hand contributed to the border of Louvre RF 5271; do not attribute the entire decoration to Fouquet. Source: https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl020214911
