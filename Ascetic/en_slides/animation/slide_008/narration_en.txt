This painting also shows how research can change an artwork’s identity. In two thousand eight, it changed hands as a work attributed to Ghirlandaio’s workshop.
Cleaning and further study brought renewed attention. The Kimbell acquired it the following year as Michelangelo’s earliest known painting.
Imaging has revealed drawings beneath the surface, helping researchers examine how the work developed. Its early ownership still has gaps, however. We’re looking at both a religious scene and a physical object whose history scholars continue to piece together. The visible image is only part of the evidence. Childhood biographies alone cannot explain where this panel spent all the intervening years.
