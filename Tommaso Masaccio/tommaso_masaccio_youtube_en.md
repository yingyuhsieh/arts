# Masaccio: The Painter Who Gave Bodies Weight

## A. Video Core Narrative
Across a brief career, Masaccio made sacred figures inhabit convincing ground, light, and architectural space. Starting with two visions of Adam and Eve in one chapel, the film moves through frescoes, an altarpiece, and The Holy Trinity to teach viewers to read feet, shadows, gestures, and perspective.

### Segment 1 — A body that casts a shadow

**Purpose / 段落功能:** Establish the question through a visible contrast.

**Key Message / 核心訊息:** A body that casts a shadow: form gives the story a physical presence.

**Content / 內容**
Open with a contrast that can be seen before any dates are given: Masolino’s graceful Adam and Eve face Masaccio’s pair stumbling out of Paradise. Adam hides his face; Eve cries out. Their feet, exposed bodies, and directional light make the loss feel physical. A split screen lets the viewer discover the difference, then a close crop of the shadows introduces the central question: how did a young painter make sacred figures feel present in our world? Preview the fresco of The Tribute Money and the apparent architectural opening in The Holy Trinity. The recurring viewing test is simple: where are the feet, where does the light fall, and can the space be entered?

### Segment 2 — Florence and a new visual language

**Purpose / 段落功能:** Advance the story through close visual observation.

**Key Message / 核心訊息:** Florence and a new visual language: form gives the story a physical presence.

**Content / 內容**
Masaccio was born in 1401 and was active as a painter in Florence by 1422. The details of his training remain undocumented. In the dated San Giovenale Triptych, the gold ground and pointed panels still belong to established altar traditions, yet the Virgin has volume and the throne begins to define depth. Show this tension rather than claiming a sudden invention of perspective. In Florence, experiments in architecture and sculpture offered new ways of measuring space and imagining bodily weight; direct collaboration with Brunelleschi or Donatello is not documented. The joint Saint Anne panel then introduces Masolino as a working partner, not merely a foil. Move between the solid Virgin and Child and the softer figures around them to show how two visual languages could coexist.

### Segment 3 — Three moments, one street

**Purpose / 段落功能:** Advance the story through close visual observation.

**Key Message / 核心訊息:** Three moments, one street: form gives the story a physical presence.

**Content / 內容**
The Tribute Money is the film’s main visual lesson. First locate Christ and the surrounding apostles. Trace the exchange of pointing hands: a tax collector asks for payment, Christ directs Peter to the fish, and Peter acts. The fresco places the central instruction, the discovery at left, and payment at right in one continuous setting. Slow pans should follow the narrative rather than imply three different Peters are standing together at one moment. Then examine the receding building, distant landscape, shared ground, and light that relates to the chapel’s setting. This is the temple-tax episode in Matthew 17:24–27, not the separate “render unto Caesar” story. Briefly compare Masolino’s nearby urban miracle as another approach to staging sacred events in a city.

### Segment 4 — The weight of exile and the city

**Purpose / 段落功能:** Advance the story through close visual observation.

**Key Message / 核心訊息:** The weight of exile and the city: form gives the story a physical presence.

**Content / 內容**
Return to the Expulsion, now slowing down. Masolino’s Temptation supplies a clear visual comparison; Masaccio’s Adam covers his eyes while Eve protects her body and cries aloud. The bare ground and strong light turn exile into a bodily event. Later fig leaves were removed during restoration, so a modern image records a changed viewing history. Continue with three Peter scenes: a shivering figure waiting for baptism; sick people along a street hoping for Peter’s shadow; and Ananias fallen beside a scene of shared resources. Avoid treating the sick as successive images of one man or claiming Ananias died simply because he withheld money. These images make cold, hope, charity, and deception legible through postures in a shared world.

### Segment 5 — A Renaissance body in a gold altarpiece

**Purpose / 段落功能:** Advance the story through close visual observation.

**Key Message / 核心訊息:** A Renaissance body in a gold altarpiece: form gives the story a physical presence.

**Content / 內容**
The Pisa Altarpiece offers a change of material and scale: painted wood panels rather than chapel frescoes. The 1426 commission survives in fragments now held by several museums. Begin with the London Virgin and Child: the child eats grapes, the throne recedes, the Virgin casts a shadow, and halos turn in space. Keep the gold background visible so the viewer can see innovation within a traditional format. Move to Naples for the upper Crucifixion, considering the elevated original viewing position and Mary Magdalene’s outstretched arms. Berlin’s narrow Adoration of the Magi lets the camera follow a procession and the overlapping horses. A simple diagram can suggest the original polyptych while clearly marking its full arrangement as a reconstruction.

### Segment 6 — A chapel inside the wall

**Purpose / 段落功能:** Advance the story through close visual observation.

**Key Message / 核心訊息:** A chapel inside the wall: form gives the story a physical presence.

**Content / 內容**
In Santa Maria Novella, The Holy Trinity seems to open a chapel behind the church wall. Begin with the whole fresco, then follow the coffering and columns toward a low vanishing point near an imagined viewer’s eye level. Identify God the Father supporting the crucified Christ, the Spirit as a dove, Mary and John, and the kneeling donors. Mary’s gesture invites the viewer to look upward. Finally tilt down to the skeleton and its warning that the living will die. The passage from death toward salvation is a compelling interpretation of the vertical composition. Brunelleschi’s direct assistance has often been proposed but is undocumented. Explain also that parts of the fresco were covered, moved, and rediscovered; its present appearance has a material history.

### Segment 7 — A brief career, a long afterlife

**Purpose / 段落功能:** Advance the story through close visual observation.

**Key Message / 核心訊息:** A brief career, a long afterlife: form gives the story a physical presence.

**Content / 內容**
Masaccio left Florence for Rome near the end of his short career. The National Gallery attributes Saints Jerome and John the Baptist to him and dates it around 1428–29, while its artist biography gives his death as 1428/9 with a question mark. The exact date and cause are unknown. This uncertainty should be stated plainly; a story of poisoning or a definite plague diagnosis would outrun the evidence. Back in Florence, the Brancacci Chapel became a place for later painters to study bodies, gestures, and composition, and portions were completed by Filippino Lippi decades later. Revisit The Holy Trinity and The Tribute Money to show what could be learned: perspective is most powerful here because it serves an event and its human stakes.

### Segment 8 — How to look at Masaccio

**Purpose / 段落功能:** Return to the question and offer a viewing method.

**Key Message / 核心訊息:** How to look at Masaccio: form gives the story a physical presence.

**Content / 內容**
Answer the opening question with a repeatable viewing method. Look first at the feet and ask whether a figure’s weight is supported by the ground. Locate the light and see whether bodies and architecture share it. Follow gestures to read a story; follow architectural lines to understand the viewer’s position. Finally compare works made in the same artistic moment without turning Masolino into a mere symbol of “the old.” Close on the two Adam-and-Eve frescoes, then widen to the Brancacci Chapel. Masaccio’s achievement is more than convincing likeness: bodies, light, space, and action work together so that sacred history can be felt as a lived human event.

## G. Fact-Checking Notes
- The San Giovenale Triptych is dated 1422; do not call its early spatial experiment the first use of perspective.
- Saint Anne is a collaborative work; precise divisions of labor remain interpretive.
- The Tribute Money depicts the temple tax in Matthew 17:24–27; no single political allegory is established.
- Healing of the Cripple and Raising of Tabitha is principally assigned to Masolino and used only for comparison.
- The Pisa panels are dispersed, and a full configuration is reconstructed; the Horne Saint Julian belongs to a different commission.
- The Holy Trinity is broadly dated c. 1425–27, while the church uses c. 1425–26. Brunelleschi’s direct assistance is undocumented.
- The National Gallery gives Masaccio’s death as 1428/9? and dates Saints Jerome and John c. 1428–29; cause of death is unknown.
- Direct image files and reproduction rights require individual verification; neither an object page nor the artist’s death date proves image rights.
