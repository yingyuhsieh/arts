In Correggio's The Holy Night, the newborn Christ becomes the scene's radiant center. Discover how this luminous Nativity turns light into a story of tenderness, astonishment, and human responses to the divine.

Our Famous Artists explores La Notte, also known as The Adoration of the Shepherds, from the glowing infant and Mary's illuminated face to the woman shielding her eyes. Strong contrasts coexist with softly blended edges, while twisting figures, a diagonal staff, and descending angels invite the eye to travel through the composition. A comparison with Correggio's Parma Cathedral dome reveals his fascination with bodies moving between earthly space and the heavens.

The painting's light also opens a deeper historical story. Luke's Nativity account does not describe the infant's body glowing. Earlier non-canonical writing, the visionary tradition of Saint Bridget of Sweden, and northern European paintings provide a broader context for the radiant child. Correggio gave this established motif a powerful expression of his own. A Fitzwilliam Museum drawing, possibly connected with the work, offers another glimpse into the exploration of light and composition.

Commissioned in 1522 for the Pratonieri family chapel in San Prospero, Reggio Emilia, and dated approximately 1528–1530, this oil painting on poplar panel stands more than two and a half meters tall. We follow its journey into the Este collection in Modena in 1640 and Dresden in 1746, as well as its later influence on Anton Raphael Mengs.

Mary's closeness, the bystanders' surprise, and Joseph's quiet care in the shadows bring the Christmas story into the realm of bodily experience.
