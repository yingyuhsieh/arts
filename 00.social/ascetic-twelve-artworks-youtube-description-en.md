What does “ascetic” mean—and how can a word lead us from airborne demons to a quiet candle flame? This journey began with a vocabulary search and grew into an exploration of twelve works of Western Christian art.

Join Our Famous Artists as we follow five themes: Anthony’s trials in the wilderness, Francis’s choice of poverty, Jerome’s scholarship and penitence, the meeting of two hermits, and images of martyrdom and repentance. Ascetic describes self-discipline and restraint, while hermit emphasizes living apart from others. The distinction opens up different ways of understanding the figures before us.

Michelangelo and Hieronymus Bosch surround Anthony with monsters. Giovanni Bellini and Francisco de Zurbarán give Francis contrasting worlds of open light and concentrated shadow. Leonardo da Vinci, Albrecht Dürer, El Greco, and Georges de La Tour show Jerome through bodily tension, engraved lines, study, and prayer. Diego Velázquez and Jusepe de Ribera bring us to Paul the Hermit, before Zurbarán’s Saint Serapion and La Tour’s The Penitent Magdalen take us to the boundaries of the theme.

Along the way, we distinguish chosen discipline from inflicted violence, sacred biography from historical evidence, and Magdalen’s later penitential image from the Gospel accounts. Close looking also reveals histories of attribution, damage, and conservation beneath the surfaces we see today.
