A painting bound for Florence was seized at sea and became a treasure of Gdańsk. Follow Hans Memling's The Last Judgment into the world of fifteenth-century Bruges, where portraits, devotion, and international trade met on painted panels.

Our Famous Artists explores how a painter from Seligenstadt, whose early training remains uncertain, built a successful career after becoming a citizen of Bruges in 1465. His close artistic relationship with Rogier van der Weyden provides a starting point for understanding his elegant figures and measured emotional rhythms, although formal apprenticeship is not documented.

Portraits and family altarpieces reveal how Memling brought personal identity into devotional settings. The Donne Triptych connects a courtier's family, political allegiance, and prayer; the Moreel Triptych places family remembrance within a shared sacred landscape. Calling this “personal branding” offers a modern analogy for works that also served faith, commemoration, and social standing.

In Scenes from the Passion of Christ and Scenes from the Advent and Triumph of Christ, roads and buildings connect events from different moments. Looking becomes a journey through time. The St John Altarpiece, completed in 1479 for St John's Hospital, brings a serene central gathering together with martyrdom, revelation, and a small reminder of the city's wine economy.

We also look beyond the front of a painting: a praying youth and flowers occupy opposite sides of one panel; a convex mirror links the two halves of the 1487 Diptych of Maarten van Nieuwenhove; and the Shrine of St Ursula asks viewers to move around a miniature Gothic structure to follow its stories.
