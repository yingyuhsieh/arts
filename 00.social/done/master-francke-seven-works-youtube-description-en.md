His paintings are vivid enough to recognize at a glance—yet Master Francke’s real name, birthplace, and identity remain uncertain. Through seven works, this video explores how one of northern Europe’s most mysterious medieval painters brought bodily weight, violence, sacred light, and collective grief into the elegant language of International Gothic art.

Beginning in Hanseatic Hamburg, we follow the scattered evidence surrounding the 1424 record of “mester Francken” and examine the world in which altarpieces traveled with merchants, belief, and materials across the Baltic. A comparison with the earlier Hamburg painter Master Bertram reveals a shift in pictorial priorities: from lucid courtly order toward compressed bodies, diagonal force, and emotional pressure—without implying an undocumented teacher–student relationship.

The journey moves through *The Adoration of the Child*, *The Martyrdom of Thomas Becket*, *Christ Carrying the Cross*, *The Mourning Women Beneath the Cross*, the Kalanti Altarpiece, and two images of the Man of Sorrows. Along the way, we consider Saint Bridget of Sweden’s vision of the Nativity, the Thomas Altarpiece’s lost movable structure, medieval visual prejudice, disputed dating, and why a museum’s phrase “attributed to” matters.
