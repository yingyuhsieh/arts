What if one of medieval art’s great revolutions happened inside a book smaller than your hand? Enter the extraordinary world of Jean Pucelle, the 14th-century Parisian illuminator who transformed a royal prayer book into a vivid miniature universe.

At the center of this story is *The Hours of Jeanne d’Évreux*, created around 1324–1328 and measuring only 9.2 × 6.2 cm. Within its pages, sculptural grisaille figures inhabit convincing architectural spaces, while monkeys, rabbits, musicians, beggars, and fantastic creatures animate the margins. Sacred history, royal memory, and the comedy of everyday life all meet on the same tiny sheet of parchment.

We also explore the collaborative book industry of medieval Paris, the workshop evidence preserved in the *Belleville Breviary* and the Bible copied by Robert de Billyng, Pucelle’s dialogue with the art of Duccio and Giotto, and the remarkable journey of Queen Jeanne’s prayer book from royal hands to The Metropolitan Museum of Art.
