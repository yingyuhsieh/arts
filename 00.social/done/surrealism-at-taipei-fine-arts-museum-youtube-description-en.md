What happens when an iron grows teeth, the moon slips in front of a tree, or a portrait refuses to reveal a stable identity? This journey through surrealism at the Taipei Fine Arts Museum shows how artists made the familiar strange—and used that strangeness to rethink dreams, desire, the body, and power.

Moving across 27 works by 16 artists, we look closely at André Masson’s automatic lines, Man Ray’s transformed objects and photographs, Max Ernst’s psychological landscapes, and the hybrid beings of Leonora Carrington and Wifredo Lam. Works by Claude Cahun, Leonor Fini, Cindy Sherman, and ORLAN reveal another side of the story: identity is not fixed but performed, assembled, reflected, and revised.

Along the way, René Magritte’s impossible moon tests the gap between seeing and knowing, while Meret Oppenheim’s *Primeval Venus* turns an idealized body into an uncanny material object. Together, these works show that surrealism is more than a style of dream imagery. It is a way of disrupting habits—changing an object, a body, or a visual rule so that reality can be encountered anew.
