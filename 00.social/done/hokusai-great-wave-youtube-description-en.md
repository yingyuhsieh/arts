You know the wave. But have you noticed the tiny Mount Fuji beneath it—or the three working boats caught in the water’s grip?

Katsushika Hokusai’s *Under the Wave off Kanagawa*, commonly called *The Great Wave*, is one of the world’s most recognizable images. Designed around 1830–1832, with the first edition generally dated to about 1831, it belongs to the series *Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji*. This close visual exploration reveals how Hokusai turns a small, roughly 26 × 38 cm woodblock print into a scene of almost unbearable suspense.

Follow the great arc of the wave as it frames and overwhelms distant Mount Fuji. Look closely at the three *oshiokuri-bune*—fast boats carrying fish toward Edo—and the anonymous workers crouched inside them. Discover how publisher Nishimuraya Yohachi, Hokusai, unnamed carvers, and printers collaborated to create the image, layering imported Prussian blue with traditional indigo and using the unprinted paper itself for foam and snow.

The video also explains why surviving impressions can differ in linework, color, cropping, and even sky tone without being forgeries. What began as an affordable popular print has become a global icon, yet its most powerful details still reward intimate, patient looking.
