What if a reliquary were turned inside out—and enlarged until you could step into it? Enter the Chapel of the Holy Cross at Karlštejn Castle, where gold, gemstones, sacred relics, and 129 surviving panels surround the viewer with a radiant host of saints.

This journey reveals how Master Theodoric and his court workshop transformed painting, metalwork, architecture, and ritual into an immersive expression of faith and imperial authority. Discover why the saints seem to press beyond their frames, how relics were embedded behind crystal in the heavy wooden surrounds, and how infrared examination exposes the many skilled hands behind the name “Master Theodoric.”

We also separate evidence from legend: a Prague register identifies Theodoric as the imperial painter in 1359; the chapel was consecrated on February 9, 1365; and in 1367 Charles IV rewarded him with a tax privilege for its exceptional decoration. Together, these records illuminate a wealthy workshop leader whose art helped Charles present Prague as the sacred center of the Holy Roman Empire.

Step beyond the isolated museum image and experience the chapel as a single visual system—an ordered heavenly army built to unite devotion, ceremony, and power. Watch and subscribe for more stories that reconnect works of art with the spaces that gave them meaning.
