How did European art move from Rembrandt’s shadowed faces to Turner’s dissolving light? Journey through sixteen works in “Masters of Light and Shadow: From Rembrandt to Goya,” an exhibition presented by the Fubon Art Museum in collaboration with the Toledo Museum of Art.

Across four historical moments, this video traces nearly two centuries of artistic change. Frans Hals brings movement and spontaneity to the family portrait; Anthony van Dyck turns pose and costume into a language of status; and Rembrandt uses light, texture, and age to explore the inner life of his subjects. Jan van Goyen allows the immense Dutch sky to become a portrait of place itself.

The eighteenth century brightens into the theatrical elegance of Rococo, from Jean-Honoré Fragonard’s playful game of looking in Blind Man’s Buff to Francisco Goya’s colorful tapestry design of children at play. Hubert Robert transforms ancient ruins into a stage for restoration and romance, while Jacques-Louis David’s Oath of the Horatii places public duty against private grief.

The final section enters the emotional world of Romanticism. John Constable turns familiar English weather into memory and motion, while J. M. W. Turner lets Venice dissolve into color, mist, and light—bringing painting remarkably close to modern art.
