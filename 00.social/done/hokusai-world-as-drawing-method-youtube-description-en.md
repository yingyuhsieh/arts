Katsushika Hokusai was more than the creator of one famous wave. Across a documented career of roughly seventy years, he turned observation into a public method—teaching viewers to notice bodies, tools, weather, animals, stories, and Mount Fuji through endlessly changing forms.

Follow Hokusai from the Katsukawa school’s actor prints and the luxurious poetry commissions of his Sōri period to Hokusai Manga, Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji, One Hundred Views of Mount Fuji, waterfalls, bridges, flowers, ghosts, painted screens, and textiles. His changing names mark new professional networks and audiences rather than a single fixed artistic identity.

This story also restores the material and social world behind his images. Publishers financed and marketed prints; carvers translated drawings into wood; printers controlled registration, color, moisture, pressure, and hand-worked gradients. Books were turned page by page, surimono shimmered when moved in the hand, screens changed with the room, and no two woodblock impressions were necessarily identical.
