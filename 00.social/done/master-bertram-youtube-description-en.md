Step beyond the familiar story of Italian art and discover Master Bertram, the medieval painter who helped give Hamburg its own brilliant Gothic tradition.

Working in the thriving world of the Hanseatic League, Bertram rose from a city-employed craftsman to a prosperous workshop leader and guild elder. His masterpiece, the Grabow Altarpiece, transformed worship through unfolding wings, gilded sculpture, and 24 painted scenes that carried viewers from Creation and the Fall to the childhood of Christ. Lions, deer, rabbits, tools, clothing, and household details brought sacred history into the visible world of fourteenth-century Hamburg.

The altarpiece’s survival is as remarkable as its art. Removed from St. Peter’s Church in 1734, it escaped the Great Fire of Hamburg in 1842, slept for generations in Grabow, and returned to the city in 1903 after Alfred Lichtwark recognized its lost identity.
