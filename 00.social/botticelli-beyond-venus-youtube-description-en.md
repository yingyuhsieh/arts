Follow a line through Botticelli’s Florence: across the armor of Fortitude, around the dancing figures of Primavera, and into the narrow gap between two hands in the Cestello Annunciation. In this episode of Our Famous Artists, The Birth of Venus opens the door to a painter whose lines could express resolve, desire, doubt, and hope.

We start with the workshops and commissions that shaped Sandro Botticelli’s career. Fortitude gives civic courage a seated, armored presence, while the Adoration of the Magi brings recognizable Florentine faces into a sacred gathering. In Primavera, linked hands and translucent folds give the Three Graces their rhythm. The garden holds pursuit and transformation as well as beauty, and its original purpose remains debated.

On the walls of the Sistine Chapel, Botticelli uses gesture and repeated figures to unfold several moments within one scene. Follow Moses through the landscape, then look beyond the foreground ceremony in the Temptations of Christ to find the episodes that give the fresco its name. A related approach guides Dante and Virgil through Botticelli’s drawings for the Divine Comedy.

The final works change the pace. In the Calumny of Apelles, twisting bodies propel a false accusation toward a judge. In the Mystic Nativity, dancing angels and urgent embraces give hope a physical shape amid the turmoil of Florence. We also revisit the earlier channel episode on the excitement surrounding Venus at the 1930 London exhibition of Italian art.
