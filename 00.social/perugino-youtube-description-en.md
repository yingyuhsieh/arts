Discover the quiet power of Pietro Perugino: open squares, luminous landscapes, and gestures that make a sacred encounter feel close. Explore the Renaissance master whose own achievements helped shape the visual world Raphael would develop.

Our Famous Artists follows Perugino from his early Adoration of the Magi to the Sistine Chapel's Delivery of the Keys, painted around 1481–1482. Receding paving, a central temple, and balanced architecture give the handing over of the keys a solemn sense of order. His contribution to the chapel also included Baptism of Christ, made with workshop assistance.

The Virgin Appearing to Saint Bernard turns a miraculous meeting into an intimate exchange of hands and glances. The 1495 Lamentation over the Dead Christ, in Palazzo Pitti's Palatine Gallery, reveals how restrained gestures can sustain profound sorrow. Francesco delle Opere's portrait offers a different kind of presence through the carefully observed features of an individual.

In Perugia's Collegio del Cambio, ancient figures, virtues, and Christian subjects connect art with civic responsibility. The Combat of Love and Chastity, completed in 1505 for Isabella d'Este, brings Perugino's familiar figures into a demanding courtly commission.

Behind that recognizable style stood a busy workshop. Reusable full-scale drawings supported production, while changing expectations brought criticism of repeated designs. We consider Vasari's later account alongside Agostino Chigi's praise in 1500, which specifically valued works painted by Perugino's own hand.

Comparing the two versions of The Marriage of the Virgin reveals how Raphael adapted his master's spatial arrangement. At the Vatican, Perugino's 1508 ceiling remains in the Room of the Fire in the Borgo, alongside later walls by Raphael and his workshop. His unfinished final Nativity and nineteenth-century rediscovery extend the story beyond the height of his fame.
