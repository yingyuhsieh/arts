# Verify Art Document

Review the supplied artist or artwork document as research material for an engaging, accurate YouTube video. Preserve its subject and intended audience; do not broaden the review into unrelated history.

## Review

Read the complete source before evaluating it. If the user did not name the target, infer it only when one supplied or recently modified artist/artwork document is the clear candidate; otherwise ask for the file.

Check:

- names, titles, dates, places, media, dimensions, provenance, commissions, attribution, and collection information;
- whether documented facts, scholarly hypotheses, legends, and the writer's interpretations are clearly distinguished;
- historical context and whether causal or influence claims exceed the evidence;
- organization, repetition, transitions, readability, and spoken-language flow;
- whether the opening, visual examples, and narrative progression will persuade and retain a general YouTube audience;
- important subject-relevant information missing from the document.

Search the web for corresponding evidence and useful omissions. Prefer current museum collection records, archives, scholarly publications, catalogues raisonnés, and other primary or authoritative institutional sources. Use tertiary sources only for discovery or when stronger sources are unavailable. Compare conflicting dates, attributions, or interpretations instead of silently choosing one. Cite the supporting pages near the relevant suggestions.

Treat visual or emotional readings as interpretations unless an authoritative source documents them. Recommend cautious wording such as "可能", "推測", or "傳統上歸於" when evidence is incomplete. Flag drafting notes, unsupported AI references, internal production comments, or source claims that should not appear in publishable copy.

## Deliverable

Return a numbered list of the content that needs modification. Each item should identify the affected passage or topic, explain the problem, and give a concrete correction or improvement. Prioritize factual errors and misleading certainty, then important omissions, structure, fluency, and audience impact.

Do not rewrite the entire document, edit the source file, or add unrelated background unless the user separately requests those actions. Match the document's language; use Traditional Chinese for a Traditional Chinese source.
