Michelangelo transformed it through an extraordinary illusion of architecture.

Michelangelo used a grueling technique called buon fresco.
This meant he had to paint directly onto fresh, wet plaster.
The pigments literally lock into the wall as the plaster dries.
This meant he had to work incredibly fast in daily bite-sized
sections known as giornate.
And contrary to the popular myth perpetuated by Hollywood,
he did not paint this lying peacefully flat on his back.
No way.
He designed his own specialized scaffolding, and he stood up,
with his neck craning and head tilted painfully upward for four
solid years.

The route through the chapel broadly moves backward toward Creation, but the nine scenes are not a strict reverse chronology. The Sacrifice of Noah sits nearer the altar than the Flood, although the biblical sacrifice happens after the waters recede. Spatial arrangement and narrative time must be distinguished.

[Sistine_Chapel_Ceiling_slide_0022.png]
The ceiling extends beyond the nine central stories. Seven prophets and five sibyls surround them, while Christ’s ancestors appear in the lunettes and spandrels. Four corner scenes depict deliverance. Twenty nude youths, called ignudi, sit around the five smaller central panels. Their poses connect bodies and painted architecture, but their exact symbolic meaning has no single agreed answer.

[Sistine_Chapel_Ceiling_slide_0023.png]
Fresco demanded careful preparation as well as speed. A giornata is a working section of fresh plaster, and giornate is the plural. Drawings could be transferred by pouncing or incision. Mistakes could be removed and replastered, or retouched after drying. The four-year project included preparation, interruptions and the work of assistants and craftspeople, rather than uninterrupted painting.

[Sistine_Chapel_Ceiling_slide_0024.png]
The ceiling and the altar wall belong to different commissions. Michelangelo painted the ceiling from 1508 to 1512, and The Last Judgment from 1536 to 1541. Modern restoration also proceeded in stages: the lunettes from 1980 to 1984, the main vault from 1984 to 1989, and The Last Judgment from 1990 to 1994. Cleaning revealed brilliant colors, while debate about possible losses of dry additions reminds us that today’s surface is not an untouched view of 1512.
